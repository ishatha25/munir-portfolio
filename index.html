The problem

Reciting well takes a teacher.
Most learners practice alone.

Correcting Tajweed — the precise rules of Qur'anic pronunciation — usually needs a qualified teacher listening in real time. Self-study apps exist, but most either check whole verses instead of individual sounds, or don't explain which rule was missed and why.

What Munir does differently

Munir scores recitation at the phoneme level — not just "right" or "wrong," but exactly which sound diverged from the reference, mapped to specific Tajweed rules like Ghunnah and Madd. Two modes support two ways of learning: Talqeen (listen, then repeat) and Correction (record and get detailed diagnostic feedback within a couple of minutes).

Why phoneme-level matters

A verse-level "pass/fail" score can't tell a learner what to fix. By comparing recorded audio against a phoneme reference dataset built from the Qur'an and EveryAyah APIs, Munir pinpoints the exact sound and rule involved — the same level of detail a human teacher would give.

How it works

From voice recording to Tajweed feedback

Audio moves through a preprocessing pipeline before it ever reaches the model — this is what makes phoneme-level comparison reliable.

01

Record & preprocess

Audio is captured in-app, then cleaned: resampled to 16kHz, converted to mono, normalized, and denoised so the model receives a consistent signal.

02

Phoneme recognition

An off-the-shelf, pretrained Wav2Vec 2.0 model — already fine-tuned on Qur'anic Arabic speech by its original authors — transcribes the recitation into phonemes.

Phoneme alignment

A custom alignment step matches the recognized phonemes against a reference dataset to locate where pronunciation diverged — the details of this method are part of ongoing research and kept private for now.

Tajweed feedback

Results

Built on a strong, pretrained model

Munir integrates an off-the-shelf Wav2Vec 2.0 model that was already fine-tuned on Qur'anic Arabic speech by its original authors, evaluated using the phoneme-level, detection-and- diagnostic approach from the IqraEval shared task. Our contribution was building the phoneme reference dataset used to align the model's output against Tajweed rules — full performance figures are held back for now as related work is part of an ongoing research submission.
